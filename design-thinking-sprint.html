01How it works

Six hours. One bottleneck. A decision you can act on.

The sprint is built around a single question your team is stuck on — usability, roadmap alignment, prototyping risk or customer adoption. We agree that question on the fit call, so the six hours go on the work, not the set-up.

1 Frame

Pin down the real problem

Bring what you already know — feedback, data, assumptions. Together we turn it into one sharp, shared problem statement.

2 Explore

See it from the user's side

Map the people and the workflow around your product, then generate and stress-test options as a cross-functional team.

3 Decide

Prioritise and plan the test

Choose the direction worth pursuing, sketch or prototype it, and agree how you will test it — and who owns the next step.

03FAQ & terms

Short answers, before you ask.

Which time zone do sessions run in?

All sessions are scheduled in UK time (GMT). Online sessions are set within your normal working day, so nobody on your team has to join early or late.

Can we schedule around our sprints and the holidays?

Yes. Dates are agreed with your team, not fixed in advance. The online format splits into two 3-hour sessions on separate days, so it fits between release cycles. Sessions can run in December 2026 or January 2027, which keeps the holiday period clear.

How does payment work — invoice or card?

The booking is confirmed by invoice, payable by bank transfer — choose "Lock in £695 Early Bird" in the form and add your billing details. If your finance team prefers to pay by card, mention it in the form and we will arrange it.

When does the Early Bird rate end?

The £695 rate (standard £850) applies to bookings confirmed by 31 December 2026. The sprint itself can then take place in December 2026 or January 2027.

What does in-person cost on top?

The sprint fee is the same in both formats. For in-person sprints, travel expenses are billed separately where applicable and agreed with you before the booking is confirmed.

Who should join from our side?

The people who own the decision and the people who build the product — typically product, R&D or engineering, and someone close to customers. We agree the exact group on the fit call.
